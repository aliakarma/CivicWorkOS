	\begin{abstract}
		Cities are moving from sensing infrastructure to agentic operation, in which AI agents perform administrative work and robots perform physical work. Allocation research optimizes fit, throughput, and ergonomics; labor research examines displacement; workforce rostering allocates training hours once the human-versus-machine decision has been made. None of them weighs, in that prior decision, the developmental practice through which cities form their future practitioners. We present CivicWorkOS, which makes municipal allocation a governed decision over \textit{staffed modes} (pairings of an execution mode with a named roster at declared career stages) rather than a human-versus-machine choice. Civic Automation Debt penalizes five deferred liabilities, and four constraints restrict the allocation: a Human Capability Preservation Budget denominated in qualified-practice hours accruing only to practitioners below independent competence, a Capability Access Constraint governing who receives protected practice, a Just Transition Constraint, and an N$-$1/N$-$2 resilience reserve. We formulate the city-wide program, an online Lagrangian rule with a bounded between-rebalance violation, a necessary feasibility condition, and a trainee-cohort requirement giving the steady-state number of developing practitioners the capability pipeline needs. In an exact bridge-inspection instance with illustrative inputs, reproducible by direct arithmetic from the reported values, the capability budget binds with a marginal opportunity cost of 0.0454 objective units per protected-practice hour and costs 4.34\% of annual objective value (0.33--9.60\% across the swept parameter values); meeting it at the chosen replacement factor of 1.2 forms 3.46 competent-equivalents a year against attrition of 2.88. In this domain, machine assistance (a factor of 1.69) and sharing the work with a supervising lead (a factor of 2.0) together stretch time to competence by 3.38, from 1.2 to 4.05 years, so a city sizing its trainee cohort from certification hours provisions four trainee posts where fourteen are required.

		\keyFont{\section{Keywords:} human--AI--robot task allocation, municipal automation governance, capability preservation, civic automation debt, workforce skill formation, algorithmic contestability, urban digital twin, constrained optimization}
	\end{abstract}


	\section{Introduction}
	\label{sec:intro}
	
	Urban automation has changed character. Traditional smart-city systems sensed, connected, and analyzed urban data. Agentic systems additionally plan, decide, negotiate, invoke tools, and act, while autonomous robots physically execute those decisions in public space. \citet{Choi2025} demonstrate AI-agent-based urban digital twins operating over live municipal data, and \citet{Shariatpour2026} describe the resulting shift toward ``agentic cities'' in which urban infrastructures sense, learn, predict, and act.
	
	This creates a distributional question that allocation mechanisms do not yet address. A bridge inspection may be performed by a licensed inspector, a computer-vision agent, an autonomous robot, or a mixed team; a citizen service request may be handled by a public employee, an AI agent, or a human supervised by one; and sanitation, maintenance, transport, logistics, emergency response, and administration decompose in the same way. Each such decision gives work to some parties and withdraws it from others, and we know of no municipal allocation mechanism that records who bears the loss.

	The policy problem is not whether AI and robots will replace municipal workers, but how a city should continuously decide which tasks are automated, augmented, reserved for humans, or performed by hybrid teams, and who performs the work that remains. We treat the second part, who performs the remaining work, as part of the allocation decision rather than its by-product. Automation exposure is distributed unevenly~\citep{AcemogluRestrepo2020,Eloundou2024}, and nothing ensures that the developmental work that turns a novice into an expert is distributed evenly across occupational classes, genders, contract statuses, or districts. When a municipality automates entry-level inspection, triage, or casework, it removes the practice through which junior staff become competent and narrows the entry route for later cohorts. Because these decisions are made one locally defensible task at a time, a productivity objective evaluated task by task does not register the loss.

	Partial automation also dilutes the tasks that remain: a junior practitioner working beside a lead on machine-assisted tasks accrues less practice per clock hour, so competence takes longer. In the bridge-inspection case of Section~\ref{sec:worked}, machine assistance and shared supervision together extend formation from the 1.2 years that certification hours imply to 4.05 years, so a municipality that sizes its trainee cohort from certification hours provisions four trainee posts where fourteen are required (Figure~\ref{fig:intake}).

	\subsection{Contributions}
	\label{sec:contributions}
	
	Most components adapt established constructions, as Section~\ref{sec:relatedwork} records; the contribution lies in the decision variable that joins them and in what the joining makes computable.
	
	\begin{itemize}
		\item A city-scale allocation formalism whose decision variable is a \textit{staffed mode}: a pairing of an execution mode with a roster of named practitioners at declared career stages. This makes distributional questions expressible in the model, since mode selection alone cannot say who receives the work.
		\item \textit{Civic Automation Debt}, a penalty for five deferred liabilities: lost skill formation, eroded fallback, diluted accountability, vendor dependency, and unsupported displacement. \citet{Bainbridge1983} stated the concern for individual operators; we aggregate it across a municipal workforce, separate it formally from the objective's operational flows, and give it a computable switching threshold.
		\item A \textit{Human Capability Preservation Budget} in qualified-practice hours that accrues only to practitioners below independent competence, with a stated unit conversion from raw certification hours and an explicit feasibility condition.
		\item A \textit{trainee-cohort requirement} and \textit{dilution factor}, which turn the budget into the number of developing practitioners a domain must hold and show, for the worked domain, that machine assistance and shared supervision slow the formation of each practitioner (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}).
		\item A \textit{Capability Access Constraint} governing who receives protected practice, so that a budget sized from the incumbent workforce reproduces its composition only by recorded decision, with the equality-law and data-protection analysis this requires.
		\item A \textit{Policy Digital Twin} with a rule language, status lattice, and conflict order, and \textit{Adaptive Oversight Zones} with specified transition predicates.
		\item A city-wide assignment program, an online Lagrangian approximation with a bounded between-rebalance violation, and a bridge-inspection allocation computed end to end by exact arithmetic, with a sensitivity sweep and two parameter shocks.
		\item An evaluation protocol specified in full, with ten pre-specified criteria: eight hypotheses with refutation criteria and two benchmarks calibrated against the worked example. It has not been executed, and no claim here rests on it.
	\end{itemize}
	
	Sections~\ref{sec:relatedwork} to~\ref{sec:discussion} give, in order, the related work and research questions, the framework's layers, the program and formal results, governance and legality, the worked allocation, and the discussion, protocol, and limitations.
	
	\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
	\label{sec:relatedwork}

	This section states our positioning; Appendix~\ref{app:rw} gives the survey work by work.

	\subsection{Task Allocation and Workforce Scheduling}
	\label{sec:rw-alloc}

	Capability-based allocation matches task requirements to agent capabilities, so that allocation follows task--agent fit rather than automation for its own sake~\citep{Ranz2017,Dorri2018}. Later work added ergonomics~\citep{Makrini2019,Merlo2023}, human preferences~\citep{Noormohammadi2025}, and measured trust~\citep{Ali2022}, with reviews covering robot fleets~\citep{MeseguerValenzuela2025} and multi-agent reinforcement learning~\citep{Hady2025}; the closest city-scale treatment allocates sensing tasks across a mobile crowd as a graph-structured assignment~\citep{Zhao2023}. All of it optimizes an operational team's performance. None asks whether repeated local decisions erode the experience that sustains future expertise, or whose expertise erodes first.

	The capability budget of Section~\ref{sec:hcpb} belongs to the long operations-research tradition of constrained training-hour allocation rather than departing from it: nurse rostering carries skill mix, supervision ratios, and qualification maintenance as hard constraints~\citep{Burke2004}, and personnel scheduling includes cross-training variants~\citep{VandenBergh2013}. Two differences matter. First, traditional rostering assumes the task exists and asks who covers it, whereas our decision is prior (determining whether a human performs it at all, and thus whether developmental content exists to allocate). Second, rostering uses qualification requirements to keep today's coverage feasible, whereas our budget is sized from attrition to form the practitioners who will replace those leaving, and the objective also carries the deferred liabilities of Civic Automation Debt.

	\subsection{Automation, Skill Erosion, and Capability Formation}
	\label{sec:rw-skill}

	Automating the routine components of an operational task degrades human intervention capabilities~\citep{Bainbridge1983}, a mechanism parameterized in our fallback erosion penalty $D^{fall}$. The out-of-the-loop problem~\citep{EndsleyKiris1995}, automation failure taxonomies~\citep{ParasuramanRiley1997}, and formal levels-of-automation scales~\citep{SheridanVerplank1978,Parasuraman2000} inform our adaptive oversight tiering (Zones Z1--Z4), while \citet{Shneiderman2020} argues that automation and human control operate along independent dimensions. \citeauthor{Zuboff1988}'s~\citeyearpar{Zuboff1988} distinction between automating and informating work separates tasks that displace practitioners from those that build competence. Skill-decay meta-analyses~\citep{Arthur1998,TatelAckerman2025} measure retention as a function of the practice interval, and would calibrate $D^{skill}$ if it were extended with a retention term (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).

	CivicWorkOS therefore does not claim to have discovered skill erosion; \citet{Rashidi2026} argues that AI is the first technology able to automate the entry-level cognitive work through which professional judgment is formed. That literature works at the scale of the individual operator, where \citet{Bainbridge1983} showed that the ability to intervene decays separately from routine competence; our question, whether a city's expertise pipeline is drawn down across thousands of independent decisions, is municipal, and $D^{fall}$ carries that separation into it. The debt framing has a source too: technical debt as the deferred cost of an expedient choice~\citep{Cunningham1992,Sculley2015}, here an institutional liability deferred to future municipal administrations, absent from the operational ledger that authorized it.

	\subsection{Fair Division and Algorithmic Due Process}
	\label{sec:rw-fair}

	Protected practice is a scarce, unequally distributed good, placing the Capability Access Constraint inside the fair-division literature~\citep{Bouveret2016,Caragiannis2019}. We use a share constraint against a policy-set target rather than an envy-based criterion because a panel must defend a distribution against a statutory equality duty, not agents' valuations, and a Nash-welfare objective would need worker preferences that no city records and workers would report strategically. The cost is that our constraint offers no envy guarantee.

	A framework that weighs civic costs must also show its work, or the weighting is unfalsifiable in the way opaque public-sector scoring is~\citep{Eubanks2018}. Technological due process requires notice, a record, and a route to challenge~\citep{Citron2008,CitronPasquale2014}, and a right to human intervention is empty unless a human can alter the outcome~\citep{Almada2019,Vaccaro2019}. Of the contestability features \citet{Alfrink2023} synthesize, Section~\ref{sec:contest} implements the evidentiary record, the intervention request, and challenge to the rules themselves; Article~22 GDPR~\citep{GDPR2016} sets the EU floor.

	\subsection{Algorithmic Management, Labor, and Who Bears the Cost}
	\label{sec:rw-labor}

	AI is becoming a collaborator~\citep{HAT2026} and a manager: algorithmic management already allocates work, issues instructions, and monitors performance~\citep{OECDAM2025}, with gains in consistency set against autonomy, privacy, accountability, and discrimination concerns~\citep{OECD2026Policy} that persist even where automation performs well~\citep{Wischnewski2023}. Municipalities differ because public services are constituted by the discretionary judgment of street-level bureaucrats~\citep{Lipsky2010}, so removing discretion changes what the service is. Ethical frameworks~\citep{Callari2024}, the EU AI Act's oversight requirement~\citep{EUAIAct2024}, and the ISO~10218 series~\citep{ISO2025,ISO2025b} specify principles or physical constraints, not a mechanism that turns them into real-time decisions about who performs a task.

	On employment, the task-based framework~\citep{AutorLevyMurnane2003} underpins the argument that displacement is overstated when complementarities are ignored~\citep{Autor2015}, and occupation-level exposure estimates~\citep{FreyOsborne2017} fall roughly fivefold when re-measured at task level~\citep{Arntz2016}; only task redesign is actionable by an allocation mechanism. Language models widen exposure~\citep{Eloundou2024}, robot adoption lowers employment and wages for lower-skilled workers~\citep{AcemogluRestrepo2020}, and platform workers bear their exposure outside the protections municipal employment presumes~\citep{Wood2019,DeStefano2016}. Residents face a different exposure: automated public-service decisions concentrate their errors on the poorest~\citep{Eubanks2018}. The ILO just-transition guidelines specify what an institutional response must contain~\citep{ILO2015}, alongside OECD policy~\citep{OECD2026Policy}, employer reskilling forecasts~\citep{WEF2025}, and Arab-region projections that Section~\ref{sec:gcc} takes up~\citep{ILOESCWA2026}.

	We use \textit{capability} in the sense of \citet{Sen1999} and \citet{Nussbaum2011}, the real freedom to do and to be that a developmental task confers and an automated one withholds, and the budget takes the institutional form \citet{Ostrom1990} identifies for renewable but depletable shared resources: monitored, bounded, locally governed appropriation rules. We borrow their concepts, not a decision procedure, which none of them was meant to supply.

	\subsection{Agentic-City Governance and Urban Digital Twins}
	\label{sec:rw-govern}

	Public-space robot policy concentrates on safety and privacy over wider wellbeing~\citep{Mintrom2025}; co-creation and transparent civic platforms raise measured trust in municipal AI~\citep{Phothong2026,NechesovRuponen2024}, and the workforce's own digital readiness conditions reform~\citep{Aldhi2025}. City digital twins already assist policy decisions~\citep{Temple2024}, and \citet{DiazSarachaga2025} supplies criteria for whether a deployed twin is well governed, the closest antecedent to our Policy Digital Twin. We ask the converse: whether governance can be represented inside the twin as an executable constraint, carrying the social dimensions twins usually omit~\citep{BenDaya2026,Qanazi2025}.

	The resilience reserve of Section~\ref{sec:layer5} draws on a literature that uses the term inconsistently~\citep{Mentges2023}; response networks recover faster when post-failure reassignment is planned in advance~\citep{Lian2025}, which is what the reserve plans.

	Five gaps follow. They are gaps of integration more than of method: each construction we use has a precedent above, and what is missing is their joining at municipal scale. First, allocation models and policy frameworks exist separately, and we know of no city-scale mechanism that makes policy an active constraint of allocation. Second, the single-operator mechanism of skill erosion has not been aggregated to a municipal workforce and entered into allocation.
	
	Third, and most consequentially, rostering decides who works once the work is assigned to humans, but no mechanism we know of that makes the prior human-versus-machine decision also asks who receives the developmental work that decision preserves. We expect a preservation rule calibrated on the current workforce to reproduce its composition (gender balance, credentialing routes, neighborhoods, and the ratio of permanent to contracted staff), so that a rule meant to protect expertise would also keep out those not already represented.

	Fourth, allocation needs a measure of whether enough human capability remains to recover essential services when AI, platforms, or robots fail. Fifth, automation rules are static compliance checks, whereas the acceptable degree of automation varies with sector, risk, labor conditions, and observed performance. These gaps motivate seven research questions: the first gap motivates RQ1 and RQ5, the second RQ2 and RQ3, the third RQ4, the fourth RQ6, and the fifth RQ7. Section~\ref{sec:whatworked} states which are answered, and how.
	
	\begin{description}
		\item[RQ1] How can urban tasks be allocated among humans, AI agents, robots, and hybrid teams to satisfy both operational and civic objectives?
		\item[RQ2] Can Civic Automation Debt represent long-term capability losses without double-counting immediate outcomes?
		\item[RQ3] Can a capability budget sustain expertise pipelines without fixed quotas, and at what cost?
		\item[RQ4] Who receives protected developmental work, and can an access constraint prevent preservation from reproducing the incumbent workforce?
		\item[RQ5] How can governance requirements become machine-checkable constraints with an operable contestability procedure?
		\item[RQ6] Can a resilience reserve preserve critical services under automation failure?
		\item[RQ7] How should oversight zones respond to performance, incidents, workforce capability, and feedback?
	\end{description}
	
	\section{The CivicWorkOS Framework}
	\label{sec:framework}

	\subsection{Problem Statement, Staffed Modes, and Civic Automation Debt}
	\label{sec:problem}

	Appendix~\ref{app:proofs-notation} collects the notation. Consider a city containing urban tasks $\mathcal{T}=\{T_1,\ldots,T_n\}$ and a heterogeneous workforce of humans ($H$), software AI agents ($A$), physical robots ($R$), and their hybrid combinations, giving the execution-mode set $\mathcal{M}$ defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:modeset}:
	\begin{equation}
		\mathcal{M}=\{H,\,A,\,R,\,H\!+\!A,\,H\!+\!R,\,A\!+\!R,\,H\!+\!A\!+\!R\}.
		\label{eq:modeset}
	\end{equation}
	Each task belongs to one critical service $\sigma(i)\in\mathcal{S}$ and one capability domain $k(i)\in\mathcal{K}$, both taken from the municipal service catalog.

	\subsubsection*{Practitioners, Career Stage, and Staffed Modes}

	Selecting a mode alone cannot express who receives the work, so we make the human staffing explicit. Let $\mathcal{W}_k$ be the practitioners on the municipal payroll qualified in domain $k$ (Section~\ref{sec:whowins} discusses those outside it), each carrying an accumulated qualified-practice total $c_w(t)$ and facing a competence threshold $h_k$, which gives the \textit{career stage} of Equation~\eqref{eq:stage}:
	\begin{equation}
		\mathrm{stage}_k(w,t)=
		\begin{cases}
			\textit{developing}, & c_w(t) < h_k,\\
			\textit{competent}, & c_w(t) \geq h_k .
		\end{cases}
		\label{eq:stage}
	\end{equation}
	Only competent practitioners may hold roles carrying statutory sign-off. $N_k(t)$ counts competent practitioners in domain $k$ and $N^{dev}_k(t)$ the developing ones.

	A mode $m$ specifies $n_m \geq 0$ human slots. A \textit{roster} $a$ assigns those slots to distinct available practitioners with a work split $\omega_{a,w}\geq 0$, $\sum_{w\in a}\omega_{a,w}=1$; modes with no human slot admit only the null roster. The pair $(m,a)$ is a \textit{staffed mode}, and the decision variable is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:decisionvar}:
	\begin{equation}
		x_{i,m,a}\in\{0,1\},\qquad
		\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}\ \sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i,m}} x_{i,m,a}=1 \quad \forall i,
		\label{eq:decisionvar}
	\end{equation}
	where $\mathcal{A}_{i,m}$ is the set of feasible rosters. The \textit{developmental eligibility} of a roster, Equation~\eqref{eq:psi},
	\begin{equation}
		\psi_a(t)=\sum_{w\in a}\omega_{a,w}\,\mathbb{1}\!\left[\mathrm{stage}_{k(i)}(w,t)=\textit{developing}\right] \in [0,1],
		\label{eq:psi}
	\end{equation}
	is the share of the human work performed by practitioners still being formed, and the \textit{group share} of Equation~\eqref{eq:groupshare},
	\begin{equation}
		\pi_{a,g}=\sum_{w\in a}\omega_{a,w}\,\mathbb{1}[\,w \in g\,],\qquad \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\pi_{a,g}=1,
		\label{eq:groupshare}
	\end{equation}
	is well defined for a hybrid team drawn from several groups and makes the protected-practice shares of Section~\ref{sec:access} sum to one by construction. Worker groups $\mathcal{G}$ are a coarsening of the protected attributes declared by the panel of Section~\ref{sec:feedback} subject to a minimum cell size $n^{min}$ (30 practitioners per cell per domain by default), not their Cartesian product; cells below $n^{min}$ are reported descriptively and carry no binding constraint (Appendix~\ref{app:framework-groups}).

	\subsubsection*{Civic Automation Debt}

	An allocation chosen on time, cost, accuracy, or safety generates externalities invisible at the task level: repeated automation removes entry-level learning, concentrates knowledge in proprietary systems, erodes fallback capacity, shifts accountability, and imposes transition costs on identifiable workers. We define \textit{Civic Automation Debt} (CAD) as a per-task penalty for these five deferred liabilities, each component a marginal proxy for the change a staffed mode causes in a municipal stock. For task $T_i$ under candidate staffed mode $(m,a)$ it is Equation~\eqref{eq:cad}:
	\begin{equation}
		CAD_{i,m,a} =
		\alpha\,D^{skill}_{i,m,a}
		+\beta\,D^{fall}_{i,m}
		+\gamma\,D^{acct}_{i,m}
		+\delta\,D^{dep}_{i,m}
		+\epsilon\,D^{trans}_{i,m,a},
		\label{eq:cad}
	\end{equation}
	where $D^{skill}$ is skill-formation loss, $D^{fall}$ loss of human fallback capability, $D^{acct}$ accountability dilution, $D^{dep}$ technological dependency, and $D^{trans}$ labor-transition burden, with weights summing to one (Section~\ref{sec:weights}).

	If the five CAD penalty components restated flow quantities already present in the objective, the model would double-count. We separate them by measurement domain. The other terms of the objective, Equation~\eqref{eq:scv} of Section~\ref{sec:math}, represent \textit{operational flows on task $T_i$'s execution window}, whereas the CAD components act as \textit{marginal proxies for changes in municipal stocks} whose accumulation Section~\ref{sec:cadmodel} models. Equity appears twice but as two distinct objects: $Eq^{srv}$ is the equity of \textit{service to residents} in this window, while $D^{trans}$ evaluates the transition burden on \textit{workers} over the settlement horizon. Developmental content reaches the decision through four distinct channels, which Appendix~\ref{app:framework-channels} separates and Section~\ref{sec:pricediag} weighs.

	Each $D$ term is normalized to $[0,1]$ and estimable from signals a city already tracks (Appendix~\ref{app:framework-dterms}). Skill-formation loss is the one that carries the framework, Equation~\eqref{eq:dskill}:
	\begin{equation}
		D^{skill}_{i,m,a} = 1 - \phi_m\,\psi_a ,
		\label{eq:dskill}
	\end{equation}
	where $\phi_m$ is the share of the task's developmental content a human experiences under mode $m$ (Section~\ref{sec:hcpb}). Both factors are needed: a task performed entirely by machine forms nobody, and neither does one performed by an already-competent practitioner alone.

	\subsection{Layer 1: Urban Task Digital Twin}
	\label{sec:layer1}

	Every service is decomposed into tasks carrying a requirement vector and a duration, Equation~\eqref{eq:task}:
	\begin{equation}
		T_i =
		\{cog_i, phy_i, emp_i, risk_i, auth_i, priv_i,
		urg_i, learn_i, crit_i;\, d_i\},
		\label{eq:task}
	\end{equation}
	covering cognitive, physical, and social demand, safety risk, legal-authority requirement, privacy sensitivity, urgency, human learning value, and criticality, each normalized to $[0,1]$, and the execution time $d_i$ in hours (Appendix~\ref{app:framework-task}). The task's \textit{developmental content}, Equation~\eqref{eq:ell},
	\begin{equation}
		\ell_i = learn_i \cdot d_i \quad [\text{qualified-practice hours}],
		\label{eq:ell}
	\end{equation}
	is the unit in which the capability budget is denominated. Its factor $learn_i$, like $crit_i$, is an institutional determination rather than physical telemetry, which is where the twin departs from the ISO~23247 reference architecture for digital twins of physical systems~\citep{Shao2023}.

	\subsection{Layer 2: Civic Capability Ledger}
	\label{sec:layer2}

	The Civic Capability Ledger records, for each domain $k$, the state of Equation~\eqref{eq:ledger}:
	\begin{equation}
		L_k(t)=
		\bigl\{H_k(t),A_k(t),R_k(t),F_k(t),E_k(t),\,\{E_{k,g}(t)\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}},\,
		\{c_w(t)\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}_k}\bigr\},
		\label{eq:ledger}
	\end{equation}
	comprising available human, AI, and robotic capability, fallback capacity $F_k$, the expertise pipeline $E_k$ and its decomposition by worker group, without which the access constraint cannot be evaluated, and the per-practitioner totals that~\eqref{eq:stage} reads. Fallback capacity is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:fallback}:
	\begin{equation}
		F_k(t) = \sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_k} \zeta_w(t)\,\mathbb{1}\!\left[\mathrm{stage}_k(w,t)=\textit{competent}\right],
		\label{eq:fallback}
	\end{equation}
	the qualified hours deployable under surge within a service's recovery window, with $\zeta_w(t)$ practitioner $w$'s surge-deployable hours net of committed work. Because it counts only competent practitioners, $F_k$ falls whenever they leave without being replaced from the pipeline.

	\subsection{Layer 3: Policy Digital Twin}
	\label{sec:policytwin}

	The Policy Digital Twin converts laws, rules, collective agreements, and local priorities into machine-checkable constraints. A policy rule is the tuple of Equation~\eqref{eq:rule}:
	\begin{equation}
		\rho = \langle\, \mathrm{scope},\ \mathrm{guard},\ \mathrm{status},\ \mathrm{authority},\ \mathrm{validity},\ \mathrm{provenance} \,\rangle,
		\label{eq:rule}
	\end{equation}
	where the scope is a conjunction of catalog predicates, the guard a decidable predicate over the task vector, roster, and ledger state, the authority ranks the source from statute down to operational guidance (it governs amendment, not evaluation; Appendix~\ref{app:framework-conflict}), the validity is a time interval, and the provenance names the instrument and the panel decision that encoded it. Statuses form the totally ordered lattice of Equation~\eqref{eq:lattice},
	\begin{equation}
		\textit{allow} \prec \textit{allow-with-oversight} \prec \textit{restrict} \prec \textit{prohibit}.
		\label{eq:lattice}
	\end{equation}
	Policy is evaluated in two stages, so that rules about modes can be checked before any roster exists: a mode-level status
	\begin{equation}
		\mathcal{P}_{mode}(T_i,m,t) \;=\; \bigsqcup_{\rho\, \in\, \mathrm{fires}_{mode}(T_i,m,t)} \mathrm{status}(\rho),
		\label{eq:policymode}
	\end{equation}
	evaluated before roster assembly, and a roster-level status
	\begin{equation}
		\mathcal{G}_{roster}(T_i,m,a,t) \;=\; \bigsqcup_{\rho\, \in\, \mathrm{fires}_{roster}(T_i,m,a,t)} \mathrm{status}(\rho),
		\label{eq:policyroster}
	\end{equation}
	evaluated for each candidate roster in $\mathcal{A}_{i,m}$. The joint status for staffed mode $(m,a)$ is then the lattice join of Equation~\eqref{eq:policy}:
	\begin{equation}
		\mathcal{P}(T_i,m,a,t) \;=\; \mathcal{P}_{mode}(T_i,m,t) \;\sqcup\; \mathcal{G}_{roster}(T_i,m,a,t)
		\;\in\;\{\textit{allow},\textit{allow-with-oversight},\textit{restrict},\textit{prohibit}\}.
		\label{eq:policy}
	\end{equation}
	The most restrictive applicable rule therefore takes precedence, which is the appropriate default for a public body. Because the join ignores the source of a rule, no rule can relax another, whatever its authority; relaxing a rule means amending or retiring it through the panel. The two intermediate statuses are executable: \textit{allow-with-oversight} binds a named reviewer to the staffed mode and adds the reviewer's time and retained judgment to its terms, while \textit{restrict} narrows $\mathcal{A}_{i,m}$ to rosters meeting a minimum human share or stage requirement, which is how a supervision ratio enters the model. Appendix~\ref{app:framework-policy} gives the conflict order, the consistency check, and a worked encoding of Article~14 of the EU AI Act~\citep{EUAIAct2024}; Section~\ref{sec:contest} makes the twin's decisions contestable.

	\subsection{Layer 4: Human Capability Preservation Budget}
	\label{sec:hcpb}

	The Human Capability Preservation Budget (HCPB) reserves a minimum volume of developmental human practice, denominated throughout in \textit{qualified-practice hours per budget period} $\Delta T$, taken as one year, so that both sides of the constraint are in one auditable unit. The \textit{human developmental share} of mode $m$ is given in Equation~\eqref{eq:phi}:
	\begin{equation}
		\phi_m \in [0,1], \qquad
		\phi_H = 1,\quad \phi_A = \phi_R = \phi_{A+R} = 0,
		\label{eq:phi}
	\end{equation}
	with hybrid values set per domain from an assessment of how much of the task's judgment a human still exercises. The HCPB constraint is Equation~\eqref{eq:hcpb}:
	\begin{equation}
		\Lambda_k(\Delta T)\;=\;\sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_k(\Delta T)}\ \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}}\ \sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i,m}}
		x_{i,m,a}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a \;\geq\; B_k(t),
		\label{eq:hcpb}
	\end{equation}
	both sides in hours delivered to practitioners below competence. The factor $\psi_a$ restricts credit to practitioners still being formed: without it a domain that gives every task to its incumbents satisfies a budget sized from novice formation while forming nobody. The budget is estimated in Equation~\eqref{eq:hcpb-estimator}:
	\begin{equation}
		B_k(t) = \max\!\Big(B_k^{min},\ \eta_k \, N_k(t)\, r_k(t)\, h_k\Big),
		\label{eq:hcpb-estimator}
	\end{equation}
	where $r_k(t)$ is the fraction of competent practitioners lost per period, $h_k$ the qualified-practice hours that bring one developing practitioner to competence, $\eta_k$ a policy-set replacement factor ($\eta_k>1$ builds margin, $\eta_k<1$ deliberately lets a domain shrink), and $B_k^{min}$ an hours floor. Certification schemes count \textit{raw clock hours} $h^{raw}_k$ of logged practice. We read these as hours in which the candidate performs all of the human work of the task without machine assistance, a competent supervisor signing off; such hours accrue the mean learning value $\overline{learn}_k$ per clock hour, so Equation~\eqref{eq:hconv} converts between them:
	\begin{equation}
		h_k \;=\; \overline{learn}_k \cdot h^{raw}_k .
		\label{eq:hconv}
	\end{equation}
	A practitioner who works on machine-assisted tasks, or shares the human work with a lead, accrues less than $\overline{learn}_k$ per clock hour, which is why realized formation takes longer than certification hours imply (Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}). The budget is not a jobs quota: it constrains a volume of practice rather than a number of posts, so a task may go to a developing human supported by an AI agent even where AI could perform it alone. In \citeauthor{Ostrom1990}'s terms~\citeyearpar{Ostrom1990} it is an appropriation rule over a renewable but depletable shared resource, bounded, monitored, and revised through the panel of Section~\ref{sec:feedback}.

	\subsection{Layer 4b: Capability Access and Just Transition}
	\label{sec:access}

	Equation~\eqref{eq:hcpb} decides \textit{how much} developmental work is protected but not \textit{who} performs it, and left alone its answer depends on recruitment: if developing practitioners are recruited through the pipelines that produced the incumbents, a composition-blind budget reproduces the incumbent composition for a full replacement cycle under the name of preserving capability. We therefore separate the budget's \textit{size}, derived from attrition, from its \textit{distribution}, which must be set deliberately. The protected practice share of group $g$ is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:accessshare}:
	\begin{equation}
		\Pi_{k,g} =
		\frac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_k(\Delta T)} \sum_{m} \sum_{a} x_{i,m,a}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a\,\pi^{dev}_{a,g}}
		{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_k(\Delta T)} \sum_{m} \sum_{a} x_{i,m,a}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a}
		\label{eq:accessshare}
	\end{equation}
	where $\pi^{dev}_{a,g}$ is the group share~\eqref{eq:groupshare} restricted to the roster's developing members, so that $\sum_g \Pi_{k,g}=1$. The \textit{Capability Access Constraint} is Equation~\eqref{eq:access}:
	\begin{equation}
		\Pi_{k,g} \;\geq\; \theta_{k,g} - \varepsilon_k
		\qquad \forall\, g \in \mathcal{G},
		\label{eq:access}
	\end{equation}
	with target composition $\theta_{k,g}$ and tolerance $\varepsilon_k$. The critical design choice is that $\theta_{k,g}$ is \textit{not} the incumbent composition. It is set by the panel of Section~\ref{sec:feedback} from the qualified local labor pool, a statutory equality duty, or an explicit equity target, and published with the weight vector; setting it to the incumbent share remains permitted, but is then a recorded decision to freeze composition rather than an unnoticed property of the arithmetic. Feasibility requires $\sum_{g}(\theta_{k,g}-\varepsilon_k)\leq 1$, which the twin checks on publication. Section~\ref{sec:legality} addresses whether such a target may lawfully be set.

	The second distributional constraint concerns those who lose work. With $\Delta_g(\mathbf{x}) = \bigl[H_g^{base} - \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}(\Delta T)} \sum_{m} \sum_{a} x_{i,m,a}\, d_i \sum_{w \in a \cap \mathcal{W}_g} \omega_{a,w}\bigr] \big/ H_g^{base}$ the net fractional reduction in the allocated hours of payroll group $g$ relative to its baseline $H_g^{base}$, the \textit{Just Transition Constraint} is Equation~\eqref{eq:justtransition}:
	\begin{equation}
		\Delta_g(\mathbf{x}) \leq \tau_g \qquad \forall\, g \in \mathcal{G},
		\label{eq:justtransition}
	\end{equation}
	a ceiling on the pace of displacement for any single group, following the ILO guidelines~\citep{ILO2015}. The ceiling stops the framework from concentrating displacement on the group with least bargaining power, but only among payroll groups, since $\mathbf{x}$ assigns no hours to anyone else. Reskilling coverage is a municipal budget fact rather than a function of $\mathbf{x}$, so it is enforced in the governance layer as a gating predicate rather than in the program (Appendix~\ref{app:framework-gating}). Together,~\eqref{eq:access} and~\eqref{eq:justtransition} do not resolve who wins and who loses, but make the answer explicit, versioned, auditable, and contestable.

	\subsection{Layer 5: Reversibility and Resilience Reserve}
	\label{sec:layer5}

	For each critical service $s$ the city maintains a minimum recoverable capacity that does not depend on a single automation stack. Total service capacity comprises the automation-dependent components $\mathcal{C}^{aut}_s$ (an AI agent pool, a robot fleet class, a vendor platform) and the human contribution of the domains serving $s$, Equation~\eqref{eq:cs}:
	\begin{equation}
		C_s(t) \;=\; \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}^{aut}_s} C_{s,c}(t) \;+\; \sum_{k \in \mathcal{K}_s} \xi_{s,k}\,F_k(t),
		\label{eq:cs}
	\end{equation}
	with $\xi_{s,k}$ the share of domain $k$'s surge-deployable hours that $s$ may draw. Surviving capacity after losing the $n_s$ largest automation-dependent components $\mathcal{C}^{(n_s)}_s$ is Equation~\eqref{eq:res-def}:
	\begin{equation}
		Res^{(n_s)}_s(t) \;=\; C_s(t) \;-\; \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}^{(n_s)}_s(t)} C_{s,c}(t),
		\label{eq:res-def}
	\end{equation}
	so when the components lost are all of the service's automation, as when one AI service or fleet carries it, what survives is the human capacity in the second term of Equation~\eqref{eq:cs}; otherwise the remaining automation components also count. The \textit{Reversibility and Resilience Reserve} constraint is Equation~\eqref{eq:res3r}:
	\begin{equation}
		Res^{(n_s)}_s(t) \;\geq\; R^{min}_s,
		\qquad
		R^{min}_s = \kappa_s\, D^{peak}_s ,
		\label{eq:res3r}
	\end{equation}
	with contingency order $n_s \in \{1,2\}$, peak demand $D^{peak}_s$, and $\kappa_s \in (0,1]$ the share of that peak which must remain servable. This is the N$-$1 and N$-$2 criterion of reliability engineering~\citep{Jain2024,Ouyang2014} applied to a workforce of humans, AI agents, and robots. The allocation reaches $Res^{(n_s)}_s$ only through $F_k$: assignments commit practitioners' hours, lowering $\zeta_w$, and move developing practitioners across the threshold of Equation~\eqref{eq:stage}; in program~\eqref{eq:program}, $Res^{(n_s)}_s(\mathbf{x})$ is Equation~\eqref{eq:res-def} with $F_k$ evaluated after the window's assignments. Starving a domain of human practice lowers $F_k(t)$ over the formation period, and breaches~\eqref{eq:res3r} once surviving capacity rests on the human term and that term falls below the threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).

	\subsection{Layer 6: Policy-Aware Multi-Agent Allocation Market}
	\label{sec:layer6}

	Ten specialized agents score candidate staffed modes: five \textit{capability agents} estimate what a mode can do, and five \textit{civic agents} estimate its effects on workforce development, equity, privacy, and resilience, among them the Policy Guardian Agent, which executes Equation~\eqref{eq:policy} and holds a veto (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). The market is a single-round call for estimates, a structured elicitation mechanism rather than a strategic game, and we make no equilibrium claim for it. Appendix~\ref{app:framework-market} lists the agents, the terms each estimates, and the protocol, and Section~\ref{sec:threats} records the principal--agent exposure the design leaves open.

	\subsection{Sustainable Civic Value (SCV)}
	\label{sec:math}
	
	For each task $T_i$ and staffed mode $(m,a)$, the \textit{Sustainable Civic Value} is given by Equation~\eqref{eq:scv}:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			SCV_{i,m,a} =\;&
			w_1 Q_{i,m,a}
			+w_2 S_{i,m,a}
			+w_3 P_{i,m,a}
			+w_4 Eq^{srv}_{i,m,a}
			+w_5 Tr_{i,m,a}\\
			&-w_6 Cost_{i,m,a}
			-w_7 En_{i,m}
			-w_8 Pr_{i,m}
			-w_9 CAD_{i,m,a},
		\end{split}
		\label{eq:scv}
	\end{equation}
	where $Q$ is service quality, $S$ safety, $P$ productivity, $Eq^{srv}$ the equity of service delivery across resident groups, $Tr$ agent trust, $Cost$ operating cost, $En$ energy use, $Pr$ privacy risk, and $CAD$ the debt of~\eqref{eq:cad}. The first eight terms are flows on the task's execution window and the ninth is a change in municipal stocks; six of the eight flow terms, and the debt term, depend on the roster as well as the mode.

	A linear weighting trades flexibility for interpretability relative to Pareto-front methods~\citep{Osika2023,ZhengRasheed2026}, at a cost to the panel's authority that Section~\ref{sec:feedback} states. All nine terms are rescaled to $[0,1]$: the flow terms against a trailing twelve-month window, and the debt components against externally fixed denominators. Against a rolling window a steadily decaying pipeline would be compared only with its own recent decline, and would keep scoring mid-range (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs-anchors}).

	\subsection{The City-Wide Allocation Program}
	\label{sec:program}
	
	The constraints of Sections~\ref{sec:hcpb} through~\ref{sec:layer5} couple decisions across tasks, so over a rebalancing window CivicWorkOS solves program~\eqref{eq:program}:
	\begin{subequations}
		\label{eq:program}
		\begin{align}
			\max_{\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varsigma}} \quad & \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}(\Delta T)} \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{i,m}} x_{i,m,a}\, SCV_{i,m,a} \;-\; M \sum_{k,g}\varsigma_{k,g}
			\label{eq:prog-obj}\\
			\text{s.t.}\quad
			& \textstyle\sum_{m} \sum_{a} x_{i,m,a} = 1, && \forall i,
			\label{eq:prog-assign}\\
			& x_{i,m,a} = 0 \ \text{ if } \mathcal{P}(T_i,m,a,t)=\textit{prohibit},
			\label{eq:prog-policy}\\
			& x_{i,m,a} = 0 \ \text{ if } S_{i,m,a} < S^{min}_i,
			\label{eq:prog-safety}\\
			& \textstyle\sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_k} \sum_{m}\sum_{a} x_{i,m,a}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a \geq B_k(t), && \forall k,
			\label{eq:prog-hcpb}\\
			& \textstyle\sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}_k} \sum_{m,a} x_{i,m,a}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a\,[\pi^{dev}_{a,g} - (\theta_{k,g}-\varepsilon_k)] + \varsigma_{k,g} \geq 0, \quad \varsigma_{k,g}\geq 0, && \forall k,g,
			\label{eq:prog-access}\\
			& \Delta_g(\mathbf{x}) \leq \tau_g, && \forall g,
			\label{eq:prog-just}\\
			& Res^{(n_s)}_s(\mathbf{x}) \geq \kappa_s D^{peak}_s, && \forall s,
			\label{eq:prog-res}\\
			& \textstyle\sum_{i}\sum_{m}\sum_{a} x_{i,m,a}\, u_{i,m,a,r} \leq U_r, && \forall r,
			\label{eq:prog-cap}\\
			& x_{i,m,a} \in \{0,1\}.
			\label{eq:prog-int}
		\end{align}
	\end{subequations}
	Here $S^{min}_i = risk_i\,crit_i$ is the task's safety floor, $u_{i,m,a,r}$ is the consumption of resource $r$ (human hours by grade and stage, fleet hours, inference budget), and $U_r$ its availability. The access constraint~\eqref{eq:prog-access} is linearized by multiplying the ratio of Equation~\eqref{eq:accessshare} through by the domain's total practice volume $\Lambda_k(\mathbf{x})$, with the elastic slack $\varsigma_{k,g} \ge 0$ denominated in qualified-practice hours to avoid bilinearity. Penalized at a large $M$, an unattainable target yields a published shortfall rather than infeasibility and never compels an individual assignment, which Section~\ref{sec:legality} argues is legally necessary. The program is a binary multidimensional assignment problem with side constraints, NP-hard in general, and is therefore solved periodically over a window; this article makes no scalability claim.
	
	\subsection{Online Allocation, Duals, and the Admissibility Test}
	\label{sec:online}
	
	Between rebalances, tasks must be decided on arrival, so CivicWorkOS approximates~\eqref{eq:program} online by Lagrangian relaxation of all five coupling constraints, with dual multipliers $\lambda_k, \mu_s, \nu_{k,g}, \varrho_g, \upsilon_r \geq 0$ for~\eqref{eq:prog-hcpb},~\eqref{eq:prog-res},~\eqref{eq:prog-access},~\eqref{eq:prog-just}, and~\eqref{eq:prog-cap} from the most recent solution. The online score is Equation~\eqref{eq:aug}:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			\widetilde{SCV}_{i,m,a} = SCV_{i,m,a}
			&+ \lambda_{k(i)}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a
			+ \mu_{\sigma(i)}\,\Delta^{res}_{\sigma(i)}(i,m,a)\\
			&+ \sum_{g}\nu_{k(i),g}\,\ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a\,\bigl[\pi^{dev}_{a,g} - (\theta_{k(i),g} - \varepsilon_{k(i)})\bigr]\\
			&- \sum_{g}\varrho_g\,\delta^{disp}_g(i,m,a)
			- \sum_{r}\upsilon_r\,u_{i,m,a,r} ,
		\end{split}
		\label{eq:aug}
	\end{equation}
	where $\delta^{disp}_g(i,m,a)$ is the estimated increase in $\Delta_g$ that assigning task $i$ to $(m,a)$ causes. Because one task does not change a fleet's capacity, the resilience term is counterfactual and scaled by the task's share of service demand, Equation~\eqref{eq:deltares}:
	\begin{equation}
		\Delta^{res}_s(i,m,a) \;=\; \frac{d_i}{D^{tot}_s(\Delta T)}\,
		\Bigl[\,Res^{(n_s)}_s\bigl(t \,\big|\, (m,a) \text{ generalized across } s\bigr) - Res^{(n_s)}_s(t)\,\Bigr],
		\label{eq:deltares}
	\end{equation}
	where $D^{tot}_s(\Delta T)$ is the service's total task-hours in the window and the first term is the surviving capacity if every task of service $s$ in the window were executed in $(m,a)$. The duals come from the root LP relaxation, the standard construction for Lagrangian decomposition (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs-duals}). The integrality gap the solver reports measures how far the integer optimum lies from that relaxation; it does not bound the error of the per-arrival rule, which also depends on how stale the duals are (Section~\ref{sec:onlinebound}).

	The budget and reserve constraints concern the state of a domain, not of a staffed mode, so evaluating them inside a loop over $(m,a)$ removes every candidate or none, and a city below threshold would fail hardest in the state the framework exists to prevent. We therefore test the post-execution state and add a feasibility-restoration branch. Staffed mode $(m,a)$ is \textit{admissible} for task $i$ at time $t$ if it satisfies Equation~\eqref{eq:admis}:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{aligned}
			&\mathcal{P}(T_i,m,a,t) \neq \textit{prohibit}
			\quad\text{and}\quad S_{i,m,a} \geq S^{min}_i, \quad\text{and}\\[2pt]
			&\Bigl[\,Res^{(n_s)}_s(t^{+}\!\mid\! m,a) \geq R^{min}_s \ \ \text{or}\ \ \bigl(Res^{(n_s)}_s(t) < R^{min}_s \ \wedge\ \Delta^{res}_s(i,m,a) \geq 0\bigr)\Bigr], \quad\text{and}\\[2pt]
			&\Bigl[\,\Lambda_k(t^{+}\!\mid\! m,a) \geq \bar{B}_k(t) \ \ \text{or}\ \ \ell_i\,\phi_m\,\psi_a \;\geq\; \delta_k(t)\Bigr], \quad\text{and}\\[2pt]
			&\Delta_g\bigl(t^{+}\!\mid\! m,a\bigr) \leq \tau_g \ \ \forall g,
			\quad\text{and}\quad
			u_{i,m,a,r} \leq U_r - U^{used}_r(t) \ \ \forall r,
		\end{aligned}
		\label{eq:admis}
	\end{equation}
	with $s=\sigma(i)$, $k=k(i)$, where $\Lambda_k(t)$ is the practice accrued in domain $k$ so far in the current budget period, $\bar{B}_k(t) = B_k(t)\cdot t/\Delta T$ the pro-rated budget trajectory, and the catch-up threshold $\delta_k(t)$ is defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:catchup}:
	\begin{equation}
		\delta_k(t) \;=\; \frac{\max\bigl(0,\ B_k(t)-\Lambda_k(t)\bigr)}{n^{rem}_k(t)}
		\label{eq:catchup}
	\end{equation}
	the per-task increment that closes the remaining gap over the $n^{rem}_k(t)$ tasks still expected in the period. Admitting any mode with $\phi_m\psi_a > 0$ would let a lagging domain stay behind indefinitely; requiring $\ell_i\phi_m\psi_a \geq \delta_k(t)$ makes each mode carry its share of the shortfall, so the test tightens as the gap grows. As $\Lambda_k$ crosses $\bar B_k(t)$ the test can switch abruptly between automated execution and trainee reservation; a hysteresis band or a proportional--integral filter on $\delta_k(t)$ would smooth it, but neither is specified or analyzed here. The allocation is then determined by Equation~\eqref{eq:argmax}:
	\begin{equation}
		(m,a)^{\ast}_i = \arg\max_{(m,a) \in \mathcal{A}(i,t)} \widetilde{SCV}_{i,m,a},
		\label{eq:argmax}
	\end{equation}
	over the admissible set $\mathcal{A}(i,t)$, with the Zone~Z4 fallback when $\mathcal{A}(i,t)=\emptyset$. After execution, outcomes are folded back into policy through Equation~\eqref{eq:feedback}, the closed governance loop of Section~\ref{sec:feedback}:
	\begin{equation}
		Policy_{t+1} =
		f\bigl(Policy_t,\,Performance_t,\,Incidents_t,\,CAD_t,\,Distribution_t,\,Feedback_t\bigr).
		\label{eq:feedback}
	\end{equation}
	Algorithm~\ref{alg:scv} states the per-task procedure, including the roster construction and the two intermediate policy statuses.
	
	\begin{algorithm}[H]
		\caption{Online policy-aware allocation over staffed modes}
		\label{alg:scv}
		\begin{algorithmic}[1]
			\Require Task $T_i$; time $t$; ledger $L(t)$; policy twin $\mathcal{P}$; budgets $B(t)$; thresholds $R^{min}$; duals $\lambda,\mu,\nu,\varrho,\upsilon$ from the last solution of~\eqref{eq:program}
			\Ensure Selected staffed mode $(m,a)^\ast_i$
			\State Encode $T_i$ via~\eqref{eq:task}; $\ell_i \gets learn_i d_i$; $s \gets \sigma(i)$; $k \gets k(i)$; $S^{min}_i \gets risk_i\,crit_i$
			\State $\mathcal{A} \gets \emptyset$; $\delta_k \gets$~\eqref{eq:catchup}
			\For{each $m \in \mathcal{M}$}
			\State $z \gets \mathcal{P}_{mode}(T_i,m,t)$ \Comment{mode-level screening via~\eqref{eq:policymode}}
			\If{$z = \textit{prohibit}$} \State \textbf{continue} \EndIf
			\State $\mathcal{A}_{i,m} \gets$ feasible rosters for $m$ from available $w\in\mathcal{W}_k$, with $\psi_a$ by~\eqref{eq:psi}, $\pi^{dev}_{a,g}$ by~\eqref{eq:groupshare}
			\State remove each $a$ with $\mathcal{P}(T_i,m,a,t)=\textit{prohibit}$ by~\eqref{eq:policy}, evaluating $\mathcal{G}_{roster}$~\eqref{eq:policyroster} for every $a$
			\If{$z = \textit{restrict}$} \State keep only rosters meeting the restricting rules' human-share or stage requirement \EndIf
			\If{$z = \textit{allow-with-oversight}$} \State append the named reviewer to each roster; add reviewer time to $Cost$; raise $\phi_m$ by the assessed retained-judgment share \EndIf
			\For{each $a \in \mathcal{A}_{i,m}$}
			\State Query the ten agents for $Q,S,P,Eq^{srv},Tr,Cost,En,Pr$ and $D^{skill},D^{fall},D^{acct},D^{dep},D^{trans}$
			\State $CAD_{i,m,a} \gets$~\eqref{eq:cad}; \ $SCV_{i,m,a} \gets$~\eqref{eq:scv}; \ $\Delta^{res}_s(i,m,a) \gets$~\eqref{eq:deltares}
			\If{$(m,a)$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:admis}}
			\State $\widetilde{SCV}_{i,m,a} \gets$~\eqref{eq:aug}; \ $\mathcal{A} \gets \mathcal{A}\cup\{(m,a)\}$
			\EndIf
			\EndFor
			\EndFor
			\If{$\mathcal{A} = \emptyset$}
			\State $(m,a)^\ast_i \gets$ Zone Z4 human-reserved roster; record the empty-set event; update $L(t)$; \Return
			\EndIf
			\State $(m,a)^\ast_i \gets \arg\max_{(m,a) \in \mathcal{A}} \widetilde{SCV}_{i,m,a}$ \Comment{Equation~\eqref{eq:argmax}}
			\State Report $w_9^\star(i)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:price} to the allocation record
			\State Execute; observe outcomes; $\Lambda_k \gets \Lambda_k + \ell_i \phi_{m^\ast}\psi_{a^\ast}$
			\State For each $w \in a^\ast$: $c_w \gets c_w + \ell_i \phi_{m^\ast}\omega_{a^\ast,w}$; re-evaluate $\mathrm{stage}_k(w,t)$ by~\eqref{eq:stage}
			\State Update $L(t{+}1)$, accumulated CAD, $\Pi_{k,g}$, $\Delta_g$, $U^{used}_r$; emit to the feedback bus via~\eqref{eq:feedback}
		\end{algorithmic}
	\end{algorithm}
	
	Per task, Algorithm~\ref{alg:scv} performs at most seven mode-level policy queries, one roster-level query and one round of agent estimates per candidate roster, $\sum_m |\mathcal{A}_{i,m}|$ in all, so the online decision is linear in arrivals and the periodic program is the bottleneck. The rule is exact only when the duals are exact and the state has not drifted (Section~\ref{sec:onlinebound}).
	
	\subsection{Feasibility of the Capability Budget and the Intake Requirement}
	\label{sec:feasibility}
	
	A capability budget the task stream cannot supply is not demanding but infeasible. Proofs of the four propositions are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
	
	\begin{Proposition}[Feasibility of the capability budget]
		\label{prop:feasibility}
		Let $\Phi_k(\Delta T)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}_k(\Delta T)}\ell_i$ be the domain's total developmental content in the period, and let
		\begin{equation*}
			\bar\zeta_k \;=\; \max\bigl\{\phi_m\psi_a \;:\; (m,a) \text{ passes the policy and safety conditions of~\eqref{eq:admis} for some } i\in\mathcal{T}_k\bigr\}
		\end{equation*}
		be the largest developmental credit any such staffed mode delivers. Then Equation~\eqref{eq:hcpb} is satisfiable only if the condition in Equation~\eqref{eq:feas} holds:
		\begin{equation}
			B_k(t) \;\leq\; \Phi_k(\Delta T)\,\bar\zeta_k .
			\label{eq:feas}
		\end{equation}
		Where the estimator~\eqref{eq:hcpb-estimator} is attained by its second argument, this is equivalent to a ceiling on attrition given by Equation~\eqref{eq:rcrit}:
		\begin{equation}
			r_k(t) \;\leq\; r^{crit}_k \;=\; \frac{\Phi_k(\Delta T)\,\bar\zeta_k}{\eta_k\,N_k(t)\,h_k},
			\label{eq:rcrit}
		\end{equation}
		and symmetrically to ceilings on $\eta_k$ and $h_k$ and a floor on the task volume.
	\end{Proposition}
	
	Condition~\eqref{eq:feas} is more restrictive than it looks: machine assistance and supervision ratios both reduce $\bar\zeta_k$, and safety floors can remove the high-$\phi_m$ candidates, as they remove $H$ and $H{+}A$ in Section~\ref{sec:workedsetup}. When it fails, the framework raises an infeasibility flag, clamps $B_k$ to $\Phi_k\bar\zeta_k$, publishes the shortfall as a capability deficit, and reports the four restoring actions: relax the supervision ratio where risk permits, procure less autonomous tooling, expand the supervised task stream, or lower $\eta_k$ as a recorded decision to let the domain shrink. The next proposition turns the budget into the number of developing practitioners a domain must hold.

	\begin{Proposition}[Required trainee cohort]
		\label{prop:intake}
		Let $\Theta_k$ be the clock hours a developing practitioner works on domain tasks per budget period, and let $\overline{learn}_k$, $\bar\phi_k$, and $\bar\omega_k$ be the duration-weighted means, over those hours, of the task learning value $learn_i$, the human developmental share $\phi_m$ of the selected mode, and the practitioner's work share $\omega_{a,w}$ in the selected roster. Assume the duration-weighted mean of the product $learn_i\,\phi_m\,\omega_{a,w}$ equals $\overline{learn}_k\bar\phi_k\bar\omega_k$, as it does when the three are mutually independent across the assigned stream (pairwise zero covariance is not sufficient). Then the expected time for one developing practitioner to reach competence is given by Equation~\eqref{eq:tau}:
		\begin{equation}
			\tau_k \;=\; \frac{h^{raw}_k}{\Theta_k} \cdot \frac{1}{\bar\phi_k\,\bar\omega_k},
			\label{eq:tau}
		\end{equation}
		and in steady state the domain must hold a standing cohort of
		\begin{equation}
			\hat n_k \;=\; \eta_k\,N_k(t)\,r_k(t)\,\tau_k
			\label{eq:intake}
		\end{equation}
		developing practitioners for~\eqref{eq:hcpb} to be met, a stock fed by $\eta_k N_k r_k$ completions per period.
	\end{Proposition}

	Equation~\eqref{eq:tau} factorizes formation time into the time $h^{raw}_k/\Theta_k$ that certification hours imply and a \textit{dilution factor} $1/(\bar\phi_k\bar\omega_k)$, with $1/\bar\phi_k$ due to machine assistance and $1/\bar\omega_k$ to sharing the human work with a lead. If supervisors assign high-learning tasks to rosters that give the trainee more of the human work, formation is faster than Equation~\eqref{eq:tau} gives; if automated modes absorb the highest-learning tasks, it is slower. Section~\ref{sec:pipeline} evaluates it at 3.38 in the worked domain (1.69 from machine assistance, 2.0 from sharing), turning a 1.2-year formation into a 4.05-year one.
	
	\subsection{What the Debt Weight Does and What the Constraint Does}
	\label{sec:pricediag}

	Because debt enters the objective as a weighted term, the following diagnostic gives, for each task, the debt weight at which that term first changes the selection.
	
	\begin{Proposition}[Price and constraint separation]
		\label{prop:price}
		For task $i$ with admissible set $\mathcal{A}(i,t)$, let $\Xi(w_9)$ denote the arg max of~\eqref{eq:scv} with the debt weight set to $w_9$ and $w_1,\ldots,w_8$ held in fixed ratio, which is without loss of generality because the arg max is invariant to positive rescaling of the objective. Define the threshold $w_9^\star(i)$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:w9star}:
		\begin{equation}
			w_9^\star(i) \;=\; \inf\{\,w_9 \geq 0 \;:\; \Xi(w_9) \neq \Xi(0)\,\},
			\label{eq:w9star}
		\end{equation}
		with $w_9^\star=\infty$ when no finite weight changes the selection. Then for every $w_9 < w_9^\star(i)$ the staffed mode maximizing~\eqref{eq:scv} over $\mathcal{A}(i,t)$ is the one an objective that ignores Civic Automation Debt entirely would select.
	\end{Proposition}

	The proposition concerns $SCV$, not the dual terms the online rule adds in~\eqref{eq:aug}. Read as a diagnostic, it says that when $w_9 < w_9^\star(i)$ any departure from the debt-blind choice comes from the constraints of~\eqref{eq:program}, not the debt weight; the objective then records the liability while a hard constraint determines the allocation, the division of labor the framework intends. We report $w_9^\star$ with every allocation. Section~\ref{sec:pricing} computes $w_9^\star=0.313$ for the worked case against a default of 0.22. Online enforcement of the transition and resource constraints is governed by the following accounting bound.
	
	\begin{Proposition}[Between-rebalance violation bound]
		\label{prop:violation}
		Suppose the online rule of~\eqref{eq:argmax} runs on a window of $n$ arrivals, each task's outcome is observed before the next arrival is decided, and the admissibility test~\eqref{eq:admis} is evaluated on the post-execution state using the decision-time estimates $\delta^{disp}_g(i,m,a)$. Let $\bar\delta_g$ be the largest amount by which execution revises one such estimate upward, and suppose Zone~Z4 fallback assignments do not raise any $\Delta_g$. Then at the end of the window the bounds in Equation~\eqref{eq:violbound} hold:
		\begin{equation}
			\Delta_g \leq \tau_g + \bar\delta_g \quad \forall g,
			\qquad
			\sum_{i\in\mathcal{T}^{adm}}\sum_{m,a} x_{i,m,a}\,u_{i,m,a,r} \leq U_r \quad \forall r,
			\label{eq:violbound}
		\end{equation}
		independently of $n$, where $\mathcal{T}^{adm}$ is the set of tasks admitted through~\eqref{eq:admis}; the resource bound is exact, and tasks routed to the Z4 fallback bypass it.
	\end{Proposition}

	Proposition~\ref{prop:violation} lets the just-transition and resource constraints be enforced online, with $\bar\delta_g=0$ when the estimates are exact. The overshoot is at most one task's estimation error and does not grow with the window; it is not a convergence guarantee, and Section~\ref{sec:onlinebound} states what one would require.

	\section{Governance, Contestability, Lawfulness, and Data Protection}
	\label{sec:governance}

	\subsection{Policy Feedback and Weight Legitimacy}
	\label{sec:feedback}

	Governance is a closed loop: outcomes are evaluated by group through Equation~\eqref{eq:feedback}, and feedback changes policy parameters only through authorized processes. The system operationalizes values set by municipal councils and the panel rather than generating policy; a parameter they have not set, such as $\theta_{k,g}$ or $\tau_g$, is flagged as unset rather than defaulted.

	The weights, budgets, access targets, group coarsening, transition ceilings, zone thresholds, and contingency parameters are set by a standing \textit{Weight Review Panel} meeting quarterly and after any stress event. Following co-creation designs shown to raise public trust in municipal artificial intelligence~\citep{Phothong2026,NechesovRuponen2024}, the panel couples deliberative procedures for rights- and distribution-facing parameters with technical review for operational metrics. Every panel determination is committed as a versioned update to the Policy Digital Twin, so every operational allocation traces to the configuration that authorized it. The panel includes three groups whose interests the rest of the design would otherwise leave unrepresented: organized labor, which maintains a direct stake in $\phi_m$, $B_k(t)$, $\theta_{k,g}$, and $\tau_g$~\citep{Callari2024,ILO2015}; non-payroll workers~\citep{DeStefano2016,Wood2019}, whom the formal headcount metric $N_k(t)$ omits and incumbent representatives have little structural incentive to defend; and the municipal legal office, which keeps rule encodings in line with statutory mandates. Residents, whose service equity enters through $Eq^{srv}$, take part through the deliberative procedures rather than a mandated seat; whether that suffices is for each city to judge.

	The panel's authority has a limit that no choice of membership removes. Because Equation~\eqref{eq:scv} is a weighted sum, the panel can reach by setting weights only \textit{supported} allocations, those whose objective vectors lie on the boundary of the convex hull of the attainable set; since the variables are binary that set is discrete, and Pareto-optimal allocations in its non-convex regions, which may include the compromise a deliberative process would actually choose, are unreachable whatever the panel decides~\citep{Osika2023}. Its authority is therefore bounded by the aggregation function rather than by its mandate, which limits the legitimacy claim. Chebyshev or $\varepsilon$-constraint formulations would remove the bound at some cost in interpretability, and the hard constraints of~\eqref{eq:program} already act as $\varepsilon$-constraints for the constructs a city judges non-negotiable.

	Liability follows the zones: the decision-maker of record in Z3 and Z4, the certifying unit in Z1, and in Z2 an apportionment by the evidentiary record (Appendix~\ref{app:contest-liability}).

	\subsection{The Contestability Procedure}
	\label{sec:contest}

	Contestability in public-sector algorithms must be a first-class design requirement rather than an audit afterthought~\citep{MoonGuha2026,Alfrink2023}, so we specify the procedure rather than only an appeal-resolution rate. It has four elements; Appendix~\ref{app:contest} states each in full, and Table~\ref{tab:app-windows} fixes the time limits.

	\textit{Standing} extends to affected residents and to any worker whose allocation, developmental access, or displacement is at issue, independently of contract status, with the municipal ombudsman able to act on its own motion against a pattern (the systemic-challenge route missing from most deployed systems~\citep{Vaccaro2019}). \textit{Windows} are twenty working days to lodge and twenty to resolve, shorter with the outcome suspended where a decision is rights-affecting, and longer for worker appeals on access or displacement, which may surface only when a budget period closes.

	An \textit{evidentiary record} written by every allocation holds the roster set before and after policy filtering, each candidate's term vector and $CAD$ decomposition, the binding constraint, the duals in force, $w_9^\star$, the configuration version, and the accountable human, and is disclosable to an appellant with standing. This is technological due process~\citep{Citron2008} made operational, and recording the configuration version is what makes an appeal against a \textit{policy} possible~\citep{Almada2019}.

	The \textit{remedy} is re-decision under Zone~Z3 by a designated human who may depart from the recommendation on the record, coupled to a systemic trigger: an upheld-appeal rate above 0.05 over the rolling window demotes the class one zone by predicate~2 of Table~\ref{tab:zonetrans} and refers it to the panel.

	The evidentiary record and appeals procedure are separable from the optimizer and are the framework's most immediately deployable component: a municipality could run them on its existing allocation practice before any optimization is built.

	\subsection{Lawfulness of the Capability Access Constraint}
	\label{sec:legality}

	A mechanism that allocates scarce developmental work against a composition target defined over gender is making a decision that equality law regulates; district of residence is not a protected ground, and a district target raises different questions. We give the analysis for EU law, and for Saudi Arabia in Section~\ref{sec:gcc}.

	Directive 2006/54/EC~\citep{EUGender2006} prohibits direct discrimination on grounds of sex in access to employment, vocational training, and working conditions, and Directive 2000/78/EC~\citep{EUEmployment2000} does the same for religion or belief, disability, age, and sexual orientation; both contain a positive-action provision permitting measures to prevent or compensate for disadvantages linked to a protected ground. The Court of Justice drew the boundary for sex under Directive 76/207/EEC, which Directive 2006/54/EC recast. \textit{Kalanke} (C-450/93) held unlawful automatic, unconditional priority for women among equally qualified candidates; \textit{Marschall} (C-409/95) upheld a similar rule with a saving clause requiring individual assessment; and \textit{Abrahamsson} (C-407/98) held that preferring a merely sufficiently qualified candidate exceeds the exemption. \textit{Badeck} (C-158/97) is the closest authority, because it concerned reserved \textit{training places}, which the Court accepted where the other group could still train elsewhere. Four design features of the Capability Access Constraint are chosen against that boundary.

	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \textit{The constraint binds an aggregate, not an assignment.} Equation~\eqref{eq:access} constrains the share of a domain's protected practice hours over a full budget period. No individual task is assigned to a practitioner because of a protected characteristic, and no practitioner is excluded from a task by the constraint.
		\item \textit{The online channel is a preference term, not an absolute priority rule.} Between rebalances the target reaches the decision through the dual $\nu_{k,g}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:aug}, an incentive term in an objective where operational and safety scores retain substantial weight. It acts only on rosters that have passed the policy and safety conditions of Equation~\eqref{eq:admis}, but those conditions concern the task, not the individual candidate. The \textit{Marschall} saving clause requires an objective assessment of each candidate that can override the preference, so a lawful deployment would need an individual merit floor, set administratively, that the dual cannot override. Such a floor could be encoded as a \textit{restrict} roster guard; the framework does not supply its content.
		\item \textit{The constraint is elastic.} The slack $\varsigma_{k,g}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:prog-access} turns an unattainable target into a published shortfall rather than a forced assignment, so the access constraint never requires an unlawful allocation. The other hard constraints carry no such slack and can still require particular staffing.
		\item \textit{Targets on contract status raise different questions.} The exclusion of contracted and platform-mediated practitioners, the framework's most consequential distributional concern, calls for a target on \textit{contract status}, which, like career stage, is not a ground under either equality directive. Neither is free of legal exposure: fixed-term and part-time work are regulated by Directives 1999/70/EC and 97/81/EC, and career stage correlates with age, so a stage-based rule could be indirect age discrimination. Each needs its own analysis, but neither raises the positive-action questions of a gender target.
	\end{enumerate}

	Two limits remain. Whether a given $\theta_{k,g}$ is proportionate is a fact-specific judgment on the evidence of under-representation, which a municipality's legal office must make and no framework can make for it; and outside the EU the analysis differs (Section~\ref{sec:gcc}). What the framework contributes is that the target, its provenance, its tolerance, and its realized shortfall are versioned and disclosable under Section~\ref{sec:contest}, so that judgment can be made on evidence and challenged on the record. A composition-blind budget makes the same distributional choice without a recorded target, so there is no parameter to challenge.

	\subsection{A Second Jurisdiction: Workforce-Composition Regimes in the Gulf}
	\label{sec:gcc}

	Section~\ref{sec:legality} treats the access target as a positive-action measure justified against a prohibition. In the Gulf Cooperation Council states, composition targets over one ground, nationality, are instead an instrument of labor-market policy. We take Saudi Arabia as the instance, because its regime has been studied empirically.

	Saudi civil-service appointment is confined to nationals, with non-Saudis employed temporarily where no qualified national applicant is available~\citep{SaudiCSL1977}. Private establishments, including the contractors through which municipalities procure much of their sanitation, maintenance, and inspection labor, are graded by the Nitaqat program into bands by the national share of their workforce, with thresholds varying by activity and size; the lowest band loses access to new work permits and visa renewals. \citet{Peck2017} finds that the quota raised national employment at a substantial cost in firm exit and in reduced employment at surviving firms, borne mainly by firms with no national employees at the start.

	Read against~\eqref{eq:access}, three features transfer. A ministerial authority, not the allocation algorithm, sets the target; the ministry publishes and audits it; and a shortfall costs specific regulatory privileges rather than the operating license, much as the slack $\varsigma_{k,g}$ makes a shortfall costly rather than infeasible, though the program penalizes it in the objective where Nitaqat applies administrative sanctions. The proportionality inquiry of Section~\ref{sec:legality} does not transfer: a regulator asks of a nationality target whether it is met, not whether it may lawfully be set. Targets on gender, district, or contract status would need their own analysis under Saudi law, which we do not attempt.

	The substantive difference is what is counted. Nitaqat counts posts, so a compliant domain can staff every developmental task with an expatriate lead and form no national successor, a gap $\psi_a$ alone does not close, since it credits developing practitioners of any nationality. With $g$ ranging over nationality,~\eqref{eq:access} turns a headcount obligation into a formation obligation: nationals must receive the protected practice that makes them competent, not only hold posts. Where composition targets are already law, the access constraint makes an existing obligation computable and contestable.

	The Just Transition Constraint meets the same structure from the other side: in a Gulf municipality the population it cannot see is large, largely migrant, and employed through the contractors Nitaqat grades~\citep{ILOESCWA2026}, so a payroll ceiling protects the group least exposed. Extending it beyond the payroll would raise questions under the Personal Data Protection Law~\citep{SaudiPDPL2021} (Appendix~\ref{app:gcc-jtc}).

	\subsection{Data Protection and the Civic Capability Ledger}
	\label{sec:dpia}

	The ledger of~\eqref{eq:ledger} holds per-practitioner practice totals indexed to worker groups defined over gender, contract status, and district, retained across multi-year formation periods and used to allocate work. Under the GDPR~\citep{GDPR2016} that is high-risk processing, so a data protection impact assessment under Article~35 is mandatory before deployment. The design assumes four measures, set out in Appendix~\ref{app:dpia}: minimization by aggregation, since the allocator needs only the career-stage test and aggregate shares; separation of protected attributes from the optimizer; small-cell suppression through the minimum cell size $n^{min}$ of Section~\ref{sec:problem}; and the Article~22 safeguards of the contestability procedure, whose Z3 re-decision is the required human intervention. A city that places worker-affecting task classes in Z1 should expect to defend that under Article~22, not only under the AI Act. None of this makes the ledger unproblematic; it makes its risks documented, bounded, and open to challenge.

	\section{A Worked Allocation: Municipal Bridge Inspection}
	\label{sec:worked}
	
	This section carries one allocation through the framework end to end. Every number in Sections~\ref{sec:pricing} through~\ref{sec:shocks} is exact arithmetic on the stated inputs. The inputs are illustrative expert estimates rather than measurements; Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} maps where each conclusion survives.
	
	\subsection{The Task, the Domain, and the Candidate Set}
	\label{sec:workedsetup}
	
	$T_i$ is one cycle of fatigue-crack assessment on a steel girder span, with profile $cog=0.80$, $phy=0.60$, $emp=0.10$, $risk=0.70$, $auth=0.90$, $priv=0.20$, $urg=0.40$, $learn=0.80$, $crit=0.90$, and $d_i=10$ hours, so by Equation~\eqref{eq:ell} its developmental content is $\ell_i = 0.80 \times 10 = 8$ qualified-practice hours. A structural determination of this kind carries a statutory sign-off requirement, so under the encoding of Appendix~\ref{app:framework-policy} the Policy Digital Twin returns \textit{restrict}, admitting only rosters that carry a licensed competent practitioner. Modes without a human slot have only the null roster, so none survives, leaving $\mathcal{M}' = \{H,\ H{+}A,\ H{+}R,\ H{+}A{+}R\}$.
	
	The safety floor $S^{min}_i = risk_i\,crit_i = 0.70\times 0.90 = 0.63$ removes unaided human inspection ($S=0.55$) and human plus AI ($S=0.60$), leaving $\{H{+}R,\ H{+}A{+}R\}$. Each surviving mode admits two rosters: $a_0$, a single competent licensed inspector ($\psi_{a_0}=0$), and $a_1$, a competent lead with one developing inspector on an equal split ($\psi_{a_1}=0.5$). We assume the domain's supervision rule, encoded as a further \textit{restrict} roster guard, allows one developing practitioner per lead at this risk level ($risk_i = 0.70$), capping $\bar\zeta_k$ at $\phi_{H+R}\times 0.5 = 0.35$.
	
	The domain is structural inspection of bridges and major structures: a portfolio on a routine cycle plus fracture-critical and in-depth inspections, generating $n_k = 2{,}100$ inspection events per year of duration 10 hours and learning value 0.80, so $\Phi_k = 2100 \times 8 = 16{,}800$ qualified-practice hours per year. We treat every event as an identical copy of $T_i$, with the same term vector, rosters, and $\ell_i = 8$; the two-mode argument and the integer rounding of Section~\ref{sec:solving} rely on this homogeneity, and a heterogeneous stream could have more active modes. The workforce is $N_k = 24$ competent practitioners with annual attrition $r_k = 0.12$.

	\subsection{The Capability Constraint Binds}
	\label{sec:binds}
	
	Certification at inspector grade requires $h^{raw}_k = 1{,}800$ clock hours of logged practice, read as in Section~\ref{sec:hcpb}, so Equation~\eqref{eq:hconv} gives $h_k = 0.80 \times 1800 = 1{,}440$; we target independent competence rather than statutory team-leader authority, which is a separate legal threshold, and note that $h_k$ is the parameter to which the example is most sensitive. With $\eta_k = 1.2$ and $B_k^{min} = 2{,}000$ hours, Equation~\eqref{eq:hcpb-estimator} yields
	\begin{equation}
		B_k = \max\bigl(2000,\ 1.2 \times 24 \times 0.12 \times 1440\bigr) = 4{,}976.64 \ \text{qualified-practice hours per year}.
		\label{eq:bkworked}
	\end{equation}
	The budget requires a mean credited share of at least $4976.64/16800 = 0.2962$, and condition~\eqref{eq:feas} is met with about 18\% to spare, at $\Phi_k\bar\zeta_k = 5{,}880$. The unconstrained allocation, every inspection to $H{+}A{+}R/a_0$, credits nothing and forms no successors.

	\subsection{Pipeline Arithmetic and the Required Trainee Cohort}
	\label{sec:pipeline}
	
	The optimum staffs a developing inspector on all 2{,}100 inspections, consuming 21{,}000 trainee clock hours a year, each of which accrues only $4976.64/21000 = 0.237$ qualified-practice hours, because practice is diluted by machine assistance ($\bar\phi_k = 0.5925$ at the optimum) and by sharing the human work with a lead ($\bar\omega_k = 0.5$). At $\Theta_k = 1{,}500$ domain clock hours per trainee-year, Proposition~\ref{prop:intake} gives the formation time in Equation~\eqref{eq:tauworked}:
	\begin{equation}
		\tau_k \;=\; \frac{1800}{1500}\cdot\frac{1}{0.5925 \times 0.5} \;=\; 1.20 \times 3.376 \;=\; 4.05 \ \text{years},
		\label{eq:tauworked}
	\end{equation}
	and the required cohort in Equation~\eqref{eq:intakeworked}:
	\begin{equation}
		\hat n_k \;=\; 1.2 \times 24 \times 0.12 \times 4.05 \;=\; 14.0 \ \text{developing practitioners}.
		\label{eq:intakeworked}
	\end{equation}
	Both routes agree: 21{,}000 trainee clock hours at 1{,}500 per trainee-year is exactly 14.0 posts, and Little's law at 3.456 completions a year and 4.05 years' residence gives the same, an identity that lets a city compute its cohort from its task stream without modeling its pipeline. (The 14 also equals the stream's trainee capacity, but only because every inspection here carries one trainee.)

A headcount plan does not show this, because the dilution lives in $\bar\phi_k$ and $\bar\omega_k$, properties of the \textit{allocation} rather than the workforce. The result rests on reading certification hours as unshared, unassisted practice (Section~\ref{sec:hcpb}); if a scheme counts hours worked beside a lead, $\bar\omega_k$ leaves the dilution factor, $\tau_k$ falls to 2.03 years, and $\hat n_k$ to 7.0.

	\subsection{Who Receives the Protected Work}
	\label{sec:whoworked}
	
	The 4{,}976.64 protected hours must go to someone, and~\eqref{eq:hcpb} does not say to whom. Suppose the 24 incumbents are 21 men and three women, 87.5\% to 12.5\%, while the qualified local labor pool stands at 68\% to 32\%. No cell of a domain this small, not even the whole domain, meets $n^{min} = 30$, so a deployment would report its shares descriptively without a binding constraint; we set that rule aside to show what the constraint does where cells meet it. Assume a composition-blind budget recruits through the channels that produced the incumbents and so distributes protected practice in proportion, sending $0.125 \times 4976.64 = 622$ hours a year to the under-represented group, or $622/1440 = 0.432$ competent-equivalents against a domain-wide 3.456. Each replacement cycle would then reproduce the incumbent composition.

	Constraint~\eqref{eq:access} intervenes on the distribution rather than the size. Setting $\theta_{k,g}$ to the labor-pool composition of 0.32 with $\varepsilon_k = 0.05$ requires $\Pi_{k,g} \geq 0.27$, or at least 1{,}344 hours a year, equal to 0.933 competent-equivalents, 2.16 times the composition-blind rate. The constraint changes neither $B_k$, the weights, nor any term estimate, but it is met only if the city also recruits developing inspectors from the under-represented group, 3.8 of the 14 developing posts of~\eqref{eq:intakeworked} rather than 1.8; that hiring decision lies outside $\mathbf{x}$. The point is not the multiplier but that who wins is set by a published parameter a panel chose and can be asked to defend. Table~\ref{tab:dist} gives the whole distribution both ways. The access constraint binds with equality because the composition-blind share of 0.125 lies below the floor of 0.27 and nothing in the objective rewards exceeding it, so every cell is a determinate consequence of $B_k$ and the panel's choice of $\theta_{k,g}$ and $\varepsilon_k$.

Wherever this article quotes 4.34\%, that range accompanies it.
	
	\subsection{Two Parameter Shocks: Stability and Infeasibility}
	\label{sec:shocks}

	Two displacements are worth carrying through, because they are the two regimes Proposition~\ref{prop:feasibility} separates and a city could meet either. Appendix~\ref{app:shocks} gives every step.

	\textit{Shock one: a retirement wave inside the feasible region.} Raising attrition from 0.12 to 0.14, inside the critical rate of 0.1418, expands the budget to 5{,}806.08 hours and the required credited share from 0.2962 to 0.3456. The same pair of modes still brackets the requirement, so $\lambda_k$ stays at 0.0454 and only the mix moves: robot-assisted human work rises from 28.3\% to 94.1\%, the cost of preservation from 4.34\% to 9.57\%, and the domain operates at 98.7\% of its ceiling. This is the stabilizing direction the capability feedback is designed to have. The arithmetic does \textit{not} establish that an implementation updating duals at a finite rebalance cadence converges to this point rather than oscillating around it (Section~\ref{sec:onlinebound}).

	\textit{Shock two: a retirement wave outside it.} At attrition 0.25 the budget becomes 10{,}368 hours against the ceiling of 5{,}880, a deficit of 4{,}488 hours or 3.12 competent-equivalents a year that \textit{no} allocation of this task stream can form. Of the restoration options of Section~\ref{sec:feasibility}, two developing practitioners per lead leaves a deficit of 2{,}528 hours; 3{,}703 supervised inspections a year closes it; and lowering $\eta_k$ to 0.68 closes it by deciding not to replace the full cadre, a contraction that should be recorded as one. The binding quantity is $\Phi_k\bar\zeta_k$, the practice the task stream can carry, not recruitment: a municipality could recruit thirty trainees and still fail, because with one trainee per inspection the stream supports at most $21{,}000/1{,}500 = 14$ trainee posts at the prevailing automation level.

	\subsection{An Analytic Model of Debt Accumulation}
	\label{sec:cadmodel}
	
	The debt components of~\eqref{eq:cad} are per-task quantities; what a city experiences is their accumulation, and its shape distinguishes a strategy that replenishes a stock from one that does not. Let $p_j(t)\in[0,1]$ be the fraction of component $j$'s required replenishment delivered at time $t$, let debt accrue in proportion to the unmet fraction, and let each component approach its own asymptote with its own time constant, Equation~\eqref{eq:cadode}:
	\begin{equation}
		\dot{C}_j(t) = c_j\,[1-p_j(t)], \qquad
		p_j(t) = p_{j,\infty}\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau_j}\bigr).
		\label{eq:cadode}
	\end{equation}
	Integrating from $p_j(0)=0$ and weighting by the debt weights of~\eqref{eq:cad} gives the total debt trajectory in Equation~\eqref{eq:cadmodel}:
	\begin{equation}
		C(t) = \sum_{j} w_j\, c_j \Bigl[(1-p_{j,\infty})\,t \;+\; p_{j,\infty}\,\tau_j\,\bigl(1-e^{-t/\tau_j}\bigr)\Bigr],
		\qquad w_j\in\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\epsilon\}.
		\label{eq:cadmodel}
	\end{equation}
	Modeling all five components rather than $D^{skill}$ alone matters because different mechanisms drive each $p_{j,\infty}$: the hard constraint~\eqref{eq:hcpb} drives skill formation to one, with $\tau_{skill}$ set, by assumption, equal to the formation time of 4.05 years from Proposition~\ref{prop:intake}; the reserve~\eqref{eq:res3r} drives fallback to one; revisable zones and~\eqref{eq:justtransition} drive accountability and transition high; and \textit{nothing in the framework drives vendor dependency}, which keeps accruing linearly. Table~\ref{tab:cadparams} fixes every parameter, and Figure~\ref{fig:cad-trend} plots the result.

	\subsection{What the Framework Establishes, and What It Does Not}
	\label{sec:whatworked}
	
	Section~\ref{sec:worked} establishes by arithmetic that the constraint system is computable on an illustrative domain, that its constraints bind, that the dual of the binding constraint is legible, and that with~\eqref{eq:access} the distribution of protected practice is set by a published parameter rather than by recruitment; Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity} maps where those results survive. None of that shows any input estimate is correct: the estimates are the authors' judgment, the elicitation protocol of Appendix~\ref{app:elicit} has not been run, and sensitivity analysis is a weaker substitute for measurement.

	Against the research questions: RQ1 and RQ5 are answered by specification (Sections~\ref{sec:framework} to~\ref{sec:governance}); RQ2 by construction, through the flow--stock separation of Section~\ref{sec:problem}, though whether the debt components measure the losses accurately is untested; RQ3 and RQ4 analytically for one illustrative domain (Sections~\ref{sec:feasibility} and~\ref{sec:binds} to~\ref{sec:whoworked}); and RQ6 and RQ7 are specified, but only the unexecuted protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} could answer them.

	Nor is a structural property of the optimization model an empirical finding. Whenever a capability constraint binds, the constrained optimum's $SCV$ can be no higher than the unconstrained one, so preservation has a non-negative cost in objective units; that sign is structural. Which terms absorb the cost, and how large it is, depend on the inputs. In the worked domain it falls mainly on operating cost and productivity (Table~\ref{tab:worked-decomp}) and is 4.34\%, ranging from 0.33\% to 9.60\% over the one-at-a-time sweep of Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}. A single-domain sweep cannot say whether the system remains satisfiable at municipal scale.
	
	\subsection{What a Guarantee for the Online Rule Would Require}
	\label{sec:onlinebound}

	Proposition~\ref{prop:violation} says nothing about how the online rule performs against the program it approximates. The bound to seek is regret of order $O(\sqrt{T})$ in the number of arrivals $T$ against the offline optimum of~\eqref{eq:program}, with cumulative violation of~\eqref{eq:prog-hcpb} and~\eqref{eq:prog-access} of the same order. Close relatives in online allocation and constrained MDPs have such bounds~\citep{DevanurHayes2009,Balseiro2023,Mehta2007,Altman1999,Paternain2019}, and the small-request condition they need holds here: one inspection carries at most 2.8 credited hours against a budget of 4{,}976.64. Four features keep them from applying directly: the duals are refreshed only at rebalances; the capability budget is a covering constraint, protected only by reserving future capacity, for which the catch-up threshold~\eqref{eq:catchup} is an unanalyzed heuristic; its coefficients change as assignments move practitioners across the competence threshold; and the linearized access constraint has coefficients of both signs over a combinatorial roster space. Appendix~\ref{app:onlinebound} states each in full. The second and third are specific to capability formation, and are where we expect the difficulty to lie.

	\subsection{A Specified Evaluation Protocol}
	\label{sec:protocol}

	The protocol summarized here \textit{has not been executed}; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} specifies it in full, in advance, so that it can be audited against a plan. It is a discrete-event testbed over six municipal sectors and 803{,}100 tasks a year, run for ten years so that retirement waves and pipeline decay fall inside the horizon, comparing Automation-First, Cost/Performance, Human-First, and CivicWorkOS (all configurations of Equation~\eqref{eq:scv}) with two external baselines, Capability Matching after \citet{Ranz2017} and Ergonomics-Aware Role Allocation after \citet{Merlo2023}, under paired Wilcoxon tests with Holm--Bonferroni control over sixty tests and seven stress scenarios, two of which Section~\ref{sec:shocks} works out by exact arithmetic.

	With four of six comparators configurations of the proposed objective, the evaluation can show that the mechanism does what it was designed to do, not that it outperforms an independent alternative; the comparison is incomplete until the two external baselines, neither yet implemented, are run. The open-data portals the protocol calibrates against also belong to high-income cities with few non-payroll workers, so the evaluation would understate the population the framework fails, a bias in the framework's favor.

	The ten criteria (eight hypotheses and two calibrated benchmarks) are \textit{pre-specified}, not pre-registered; they will be lodged on the Open Science Framework before execution. Their thresholds were set with the worked example's arithmetic in hand, a weaker form of pre-specification than setting them blind.
	
	\subsection{Who Wins and Who Loses}
	\label{sec:whowins}
	
	The intended winners are residents whose critical services retain human fallback: if the reserve is sized and maintained as specified, constraint~\eqref{eq:res3r} turns an AI-service outage into a degradation rather than an interruption, though a reserve sized for an outage is not sized for a surge.

	Workers who receive protected developmental work gain, provided $\theta_{k,g}$ is deliberately calibrated rather than inherited. A capability budget rations a scarce good, and under a composition-blind budget the under-represented group of Section~\ref{sec:whoworked} receives 622 hours a year against 1{,}344 under the access constraint.

	One group is served worst. Workers outside the municipal payroll (contracted, agency, informal, or platform-mediated~\citep{DeStefano2016,Wood2019}) are absent from $N_k(t)$, ineligible as roster members, and unprotected by Equation~\eqref{eq:justtransition}. Where they perform much of a city's sanitation, inspection, and logistics labor, CivicWorkOS as specified would preserve the permanent workforce while the contracted one absorbed the displacement, and report a satisfied access constraint while doing so; Table~\ref{tab:dist} prints the zero row, and Section~\ref{sec:gcc} explains why it is large in a Gulf city. No parameter setting fixes this, because it follows from a data substrate that presumes formal employment.

	The framework also legitimates automation as much as it constrains it: a documented $SCV$ computation, a satisfied budget, and a versioned panel decision can justify displacing workers once the budget is met. Whether that is a feature or a hazard depends on who sets the weights, which is why panel composition is a design parameter and why Proposition~\ref{prop:price} lets a city see whether its objective or its constraints are deciding. The framework is not neutral between contested outcomes; it makes a political choice explicit and traceable.
	
	\subsection{Threats to Validity}
	\label{sec:threats}
	
	\textit{Construct validity.} Qualified-practice hours are a proxy, and hours are not learning; without the retention functions of the skill-decay literature~\citep{Arthur1998,TatelAckerman2025}, the ledger overstates competence under intermittent practice. \textit{Internal validity.} Most terms are reported by agents with an interest in their value, and $\phi_m$, which enters four channels, comes from the Workforce Development Agent (Appendix~\ref{app:framework-market}), whose remit favors a larger budget; cross-validation against observed outcomes is only a partial remedy. \textit{External validity.} A single modeled city of one size and a high-income calibration base say nothing about a majority-informal workforce, where the problem is most severe.

	\subsection{Limitations}
	\label{sec:limitations}
	
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textit{No measured inputs.} The estimates and parameters are the authors' own; the elicitation protocol that would replace them (Appendix~\ref{app:elicit}) has not been run.
		\item \textit{Weight legitimacy.} The quarterly panel of Section~\ref{sec:feedback} requires deliberative processes a municipality would have to build, and some parameters move faster than any panel meets.
		\item \textit{Linear scalarization.} The panel can reach only supported allocations (Section~\ref{sec:feedback}).
		\item \textit{No guarantee for the online rule.} Beyond Proposition~\ref{prop:violation} there is no regret or cumulative-violation bound (Section~\ref{sec:onlinebound}); safe reinforcement learning~\citep{GarciaFernandez2015} offers a further route.
		\item \textit{Unmeasured tractability.} Program~\eqref{eq:program} is NP-hard in general, and no claim here depends on where it stops being tractable.
		\item \textit{No envy guarantee.} The access constraint is a share constraint, not a fair-division mechanism~\citep{Bouveret2016}, so members of one group can receive very different practice.
		\item \textit{Privacy and gaming.} The measures of Section~\ref{sec:dpia} bound but do not remove the ledger's exposure, and $\phi_m$ is the most gameable quantity in the framework.
		\item \textit{Machine-checkability and jurisdiction.} Decidable predicates cannot capture the discretionary judgment that constitutes much of a public service~\citep{Lipsky2010}, and Sections~\ref{sec:legality} and~\ref{sec:gcc} cover two jurisdictions without amounting to legal advice in either.
		\item \textit{Formal-employment presumption.} The framework preserves the visible payroll workforce and cannot see the rest. This is the limitation we would fix first.
	\end{itemize}

	\section{Conclusions}
	\label{sec:conclusion}
	
	An allocation decides more than whether humans, agents, or robots perform a task better: it changes what a city will be able to do, and who will be able to do it. CivicWorkOS governs these decisions jointly rather than one task at a time.

	Its central claim is that human expertise, fallback competence, and accountability are renewable but depletable civic resources, and that automation consuming them without replenishment creates Civic Automation Debt. The concern has established lineages~\citep{Bainbridge1983,Cunningham1992,Burke2004}; the contribution is a decision variable that names who performs the work, a capability constraint that credits only the people it is sized to form, an access constraint that makes their distribution a contestable choice, and a necessary feasibility condition with the actions to take when it fails.

	On illustrative inputs, the worked bridge-inspection allocation shows the following. Meeting the capability budget reverses the winning staffed mode, at a marginal opportunity cost of 0.0454 objective units per protected-practice hour and a cost of 4.34\% of annual objective value (0.33--9.60\% across the sweep). At the chosen replacement factor of 1.2 it forms 3.46 competent-equivalents a year against attrition of 2.88, where unconstrained allocation forms nobody. Given the recruitment it presupposes, an access constraint more than doubles the protected practice reaching an under-represented group, from 622 to 1{,}344 hours a year. And machine assistance (a factor of 1.69) and sharing the work with a lead (2.0) stretch time to competence by 3.38, from 1.2 years to 4.05, so a plan sized on certification hours provisions four trainee posts where fourteen are required (Figure~\ref{fig:intake}).

	Three limits bear restating. The framework bounds skill debt but not vendor-dependency debt (Figure~\ref{fig:cad-trend}). Its feasibility margin in the worked domain is about 18\% on three axes, so a stricter certification regime or an older workforce reaches the boundary. And it cannot see workers off the payroll, whom the exposure literature identifies as most exposed.

	Future work should measure the inputs through the elicitation protocol; prove regret and violation bounds for the online rule (Section~\ref{sec:onlinebound}); extend the ledger to contracted and platform-mediated practitioners; let $c_w$ depreciate under intermittent practice; and pilot the evidentiary record and contestability procedure of Section~\ref{sec:contest} against an existing municipal allocation practice, which a city could adopt without the optimizer.
