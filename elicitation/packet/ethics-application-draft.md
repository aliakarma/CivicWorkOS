## 1. Purpose and scientific justification

The framework in the manuscript allocates municipal work among humans, AI agents and robots under a budget of qualified-practice hours. Its numerical results depend on four quantities that are professional judgments, not measurements: how much a task teaches, how much judgment a human retains in a machine-assisted mode, how many trainees a lead can supervise, and how many supervised hours certification actually requires. The manuscript states these as the authors' own estimates and says the protocol that would replace them has not been run. This study runs it, for one domain, so the estimates can be compared with the judgments of practitioners.

## 2. Participants

At least 12 adults, four in each of three groups: competent practitioners (five or more years in structural inspection), developing practitioners, and supervisors who sign off progression. Source: [participating municipality / professional body]. Recruitment is by [the body's mailing list / a gatekeeper], never by the research team approaching named individuals. Exclusion: anyone in a reporting line to a member of the research team. Developing practitioners are included deliberately; they are the only group with first-hand experience of what a machine-assisted task teaches.

## 3. Procedure

Two online rounds, about 45 minutes each, [two weeks] apart. Round one is independent. Round two shows the anonymised distribution of round-one ratings and the written reasons for extreme ratings. See `instrument-booklet.md`.

## 4. Risks and how they are managed

| Risk | Management |
|---|---|
| Ratings about colleagues' development could be traced to a rater and reach an employer | Pseudonymous rater ids; key held separately by [custodian] and destroyed at [date]; group-level results only; no employer is told who took part; small cells (fewer than 4) are never reported |
| Developing practitioners may feel pressure to take part, or fear consequences of candid ratings | Recruitment through the gatekeeper with no managerial involvement; consent form states that refusal or withdrawal has no effect on the participant's allocation under any deployed system; the research team has no role in allocation |
| Ratings concerning access to protected work affect participants' own interests | Stated on the information sheet; the study informs no allocation decision |
| Free-text rationales identify a person or a structure | Rationales are screened and redacted before the round-two summary is shown |
